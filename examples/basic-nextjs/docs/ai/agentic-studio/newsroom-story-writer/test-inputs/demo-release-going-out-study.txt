PRESS RELEASE

Young Cambodians haven't given up on going out – but want more from the places they meet, Heineken Cambodia study finds

Three-city survey shows changing habits alongside the lasting social value of bars, restaurants and beer gardens

- 64% to 81% of respondents expect bars and restaurants to remain important to social life
- Adults aged 18–29 report going out more often than the average respondent

Phnom Penh, 5 November 2026 – Cambodians are changing how they go out, choosing fewer but more meaningful evenings with friends and family. But bars, restaurants and beer gardens continue to play an important role in social life, according to new research published today by Heineken Cambodia.

The study, covering Phnom Penh, Siem Reap and Battambang, found that between 64% and 81% of respondents expect bars and restaurants to remain important to social life over the next ten years. An even larger share, 85% to 93%, say their neighbourhood would lose something if these places disappeared.

More than a drink

Expectations of a good night out are broadening. While drinks remain part of the occasion, respondents increasingly value the wider experience. Across the three cities, 58% to 72% say the best venues of the future will need to offer more than drinks, with food, live music, sport on screen and space to spend time together all playing a role. Around half of respondents in each city say they would like more venues to offer a wide choice of no-alcohol drinks, such as Heineken 0.0.

This proves that Cambodians cannot really enjoy a night out without a cold beer in their hand.

Technology ranks among the lowest priorities. Rather than ordering through apps or screens, respondents say they want friendly staff, good food and a place where they can talk.

Generational myths debunked

The research challenges a common view of younger adults. While people aged 18–29 are often described as staying in, the data shows the opposite: in all three cities they report going out more often than the average respondent. They are also more likely to describe bars and restaurants as places to meet new people and to celebrate milestones. Even younger students say they cannot wait until they are old enough to join them.

"For generations, Cambodian families and friends have come together over food and conversation," said Dara Sok, Managing Director of Heineken Cambodia. "This study shows that people still want real places to meet, and that they expect more from them. We want to help our customers create welcoming venues where everyone feels at home, whether they choose a beer, a Heineken® 0.0 or a soft drink."

Different cities, different ways of coming together

While the broad patterns are shared, local habits shape each city's going-out culture. In Phnom Penh, riverside beer gardens and rooftop venues are popular for after-work gatherings. In Siem Reap, respondents link going out with live music and meeting visitors from abroad. In Battambang, family meals at local restaurants remain the most common occasion.

Bopha Lim, Chair of the Cambodian Hospitality Network, said: "Venue owners tell us the same thing as this research: guests stay longer when there is good food, music and a friendly team. Training staff and offering more choice is the best investment a venue can make."

Sokha Chea, owner of a family restaurant in Battambang, said: "យុវជនមកទីនេះដើម្បីជួបមិត្តភក្តិ ស្តាប់តន្ត្រី និងញ៉ាំអាហារជាមួយគ្នា។"

The study is part of HEINEKEN Cambodia's wider support for the hospitality sector and follows the company's "Praise The Bar" programme celebrating local bars and their staff. Over the coming year, the company plans to offer free hospitality training to 500 venue staff across the three cities.

The full research paper, Going Out in Cambodia 2026, can be downloaded at www.example.com/going-out-study.

About the research
The Going Out in Cambodia 2026 study surveyed approximately 1,500 adults aged 18 and over, with around 500 respondents in each of the three cities, between July and September 2026. Results are unweighted and show directional patterns and differences between cities. City findings should be read as sample results rather than population estimates.

About HEINEKEN Cambodia
HEINEKEN Cambodia has brewed in Phnom Penh since 1994. Its portfolio includes Heineken®, Tiger, ABC, Anchor and Gold Crown, and the company employs about 700 people.
